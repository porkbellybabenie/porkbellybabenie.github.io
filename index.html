Job Market Paper: “Doctors With(out) Borders: Effects of Easing Occupational Licensing for Foreign-trained Physicians”Latest Draft

This paper studies how easing licensing requirements for non-U.S.-citizen physicians affects the supply and composition of the physician workforce and hospitals’ investment in other inputs. From 1960 through the 1970s, more than thirty states removed citizenship requirements for medical licensure. I use foreign medical graduates (FMGs) as a proxy for non-citizen physicians. Using newly assembled licensure and physician data, I show that easing a licensing requirement changes physician supply through expanding entry, with a suggestive decline among established foreign incumbents, no detectable crowd-out of U.S.-trained physicians, and no short-run deterioration in the average quality of the licensed FMG stock. Hospitals respond by expanding staffed beds by about 4 percent. Effects are concentrated among counties with above-median Medicaid exposure, suggesting physician supply is a binding constraint on hospital input adjustment in anticipation of a growth in demand for health care. Estimated effects on population mortality are imprecise. These effects emerge primarily after the federal government relaxed J-1 visa return requirements in 1970, indicating complementary constraints between state and federal regulation.
